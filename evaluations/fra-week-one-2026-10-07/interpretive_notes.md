The following explanations are **inferences from card rules and the observed patterns**, not proof of causes or reconstructed prerelease reasoning:

* **Ajani Unrelenting:** every model undershot a 73.60% reported GIH rate (4,371 observations). Its loyalty activations create a Cadet before the ability resolves; its −3 preserves your tokens while sweeping other creatures. This supplies immediate stabilization plus a surviving threat. Uldaros Theorix is another extreme high-end threat the ensemble understated. These tail misses suggest forecasts compressed the best bombs too strongly.
* **Countersculpt and Lich's Relic:** the former combines a counterspell with Jace progress; the latter is a three-mana removal sequence including its optional payment, followed by reusable Equipment. Both provide interaction plus durable value. Treating the primary card type as the whole effect misses their floor. Countersculpt also belongs to the blue group the ensemble understated.
* **Craftwork Crusher:** seven mana and restrictive colored pips accompany an ensemble forecast of 58.02%, versus 62.47% observed across 18,994 draws. Its entry provides two immediate effects: removal, another body, and/or a replacement card, on a large trampling body. Heartwood helps casting it. This corroborates the favorable assessment in the [September 21 Lords of Limited opening](https://podcastrex.com/shows/lords-of-limited/497-our-prerelease-guide-for-reality-fracture/transcript); it ranks first among the 107 reported main-set uncommons. A seven-mana card can succeed when its entry is self-contained; this is not evidence to raise every expensive card.
* **Way of the Necromancer:** a 53.17% mean forecast versus 44.15% observed (2,059). It enters with two Jace loyalty, below the ordinary draw threshold, and its repeatable benefit depends on later creature deaths and protecting planeswalkers. Its low immediate board impact creates a plausible failure mode. The ten-card Way group is overpredicted, but the overall 35-card empower group has nearly zero ensemble bias: the evidence concerns particular implementations, rather than supporting a blanket rejection of the mechanic.
* **Flickering Hound:** a four-mana 2/2 provides no entry effect itself, needing later creature casts and suitable creatures to blink. The ensemble's 54.93% versus 46.96% observed (2,106) is consistent with valuing a synergy ceiling more highly than its tempo cost and setup requirements.
* **Draconic Visitor and Yoshimaru, Beloved Companion:** both offer conditional synergy. Visitor is a five-mana flyer whose exceptional output requires additional artifact-token creation; Yoshimaru requires counter production beyond its expensive activation. Their observed 54.54% and 49.39% rates trail the ensemble's 61.13% and 55.35%. These cases suggest optimistic assumptions about payoff density and timing, while deck selection remains a competing explanation.
* **Community blind spots in blue:** Fblthp, Impossibly Lost reaches 60.91% across 21,591 observations; its attack-triggered draw-two payoff can matter without assembling its alternate win condition. Sphinx's Approach reaches 57.52% across 7,969: its base effect draws two cards, while the special Sphinx-search sequence is optional. The creator consensus understates both after conversion. Way of the Mind Sculptor reaches 58.34% across 21,234: its entry supplies enough Jace loyalty to draw immediately, and the enchantment adds another card when paying the draw ability's cost. These are specific examples where a combo-looking card or engine still offers usable immediate value. LR's conditional-only rating for Mind Sculptor is preserved but excluded from its general card score.

Color groups below use **the card's front-face printed colors**, not the deck archetype or deck win rate. BU represents six blue/black gold cards, not every Dimir deck. Blue and blue/black gold cards are underpredicted; red and black/red gold cards are overpredicted. This provides context for the interaction misses without proving archetype strength. Planeswalkers are underpredicted on average; all cards costing at least five mana have almost no average bias. The evidence supports strong individual bombs rather than a universal expensive-card correction. Gold-card groups are small, categories overlap, and these patterns were selected after seeing outcomes.
